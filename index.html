Identify the Variables

Correlation has no IV and no DV. You're not manipulating anything — just measuring two things and asking how they move together. Both are simply called variables.

The question to ask

What two things were measured? Those are your variables. The sample (who was recruited) is not a variable.

Write the Hypotheses

Same George Costanza logic. Just do the opposite.

H0 — Null

There is no relationship between the two variables, r = 0.

HA — Alternative

There is a relationship between the two variables, r ≠ 0.

Remember

You do not predict the direction (positive or negative) in the hypotheses. HA just says a relationship exists.

Causal vs. Association Language

Correlation tells you about relationships — never about cause and effect. One of the most common exam mistakes is using causal verbs in a correlation write-up.

Causal Verbs — NEVER use for correlation

caused, led to, improved, decreased, influenced, predicted, affected, produced, resulted in, made

Association Verbs — CORRECT for correlation

is related to, is associated with, is correlated with, tends to occur with, is linked to

The test

If the verb implies that one variable did something to the other variable, it's causal and you can't use it for correlation.

Interpret the Output

Three things every time: direction, strength, and significance.

Step by step

1. Direction — the sign of r. Positive = both go up. Negative = one up, one down.

2. Strength — the absolute value of r, matched to the benchmarks (.00–.09 negligible, .10–.29 weak, .30–.49 moderate, .50–.69 strong, .70–.89 very strong, .90–1.00 extremely strong).

3. Significance — is p < .05?

Reading a Correlation Table

Research articles often report correlation results in a matrix rather than one-at-a-time. Here's how to read one.

Key facts

The diagonal is always 1.00 — every variable correlates perfectly with itself.

The table is mirrored — the value above the diagonal is the same as below it, so only half is usually shown.

Stars (*) mean significant — typically * = p < .05, ** = p < .01.

APA Write-Ups

Every correlation write-up follows the same template. Memorize it, then swap in the specifics.

Template
A correlation analysis was conducted to examine the relationship between [Variable 1] and [Variable 2]. Results showed a statistically significant [positive/negative] relationship, r = [value], p < .05.
Common mistakes

Using causal language. Never say "effect," "caused," or "influenced." Say "relationship."

Skipping the direction. Always state whether the relationship is positive or negative.

Non-significant results. If p > .05, write "Results showed a nonsignificant [direction] relationship" and use p > .05 instead of p < .05.
